How we counted the shared dinner — and how it could be wrong

We did not work out your share from the size of the bill. We used only what actually moved: you paid RM180.00 on 7 September (#12), RM90.00 came back on 8 September (#13), so we counted RM90.00. That choice can be wrong in two directions, and only you can say which:

If the RM90.00 was not a settling-up and the whole bill really was yours, we have undercounted by RM90.00 and personal spending is 1,635.50 + 90.00 = RM1,725.50. If you are still owed money, your share is smaller than RM90.00 — and we cannot put a number on that end, because we cannot see a repayment that has not happened.

Part 3 of 4One thing you could change, with the arithmeticSynthetic

This follows from flag 2, because it is the largest line you could move without changing anything structural. It is one option, not a recommendation.

Swap 3 of your 8 delivery orders for meals cooked from the groceries you already buy.

How the saving was worked out average delivery order   287.60 ÷ 8 = 35.95  (from flag 2)
assumed cost of a home-cooked equivalent   8.50  ← an assumption, not from your data
saving per swap   35.95 − 8.50 = 27.45
3 swaps in a month   27.45 × 3 = 82.35 a month
if it held for a full year   82.35 × 12 = 988.20 a year
What we guessed — if either guess is wrong, the number is wrong
  1. RM8.50 a meal. We have not measured what cooking actually costs you; we picked a figure. Tell us yours and the arithmetic above runs again on it.
  2. Three swaps out of eight. We do not know that three is realistic, or that those particular orders were the skippable ones. If only one swap a month is realistic: 27.45 × 1 = RM27.45 a month, not RM82.35.

So under our own guesses the honest range is RM27.45 to RM82.35 a month. The RM988.20 a year is an upper bound that assumes the change holds every month for twelve months, and habit changes usually do not hold all year.

Two things this is not. It is not a claim that you cannot afford delivery — on these numbers you comfortably can. And it is not advice to stop. It is the one lever in this month's data large enough to be worth naming, and we have deliberately not turned it into a target or a budget.
